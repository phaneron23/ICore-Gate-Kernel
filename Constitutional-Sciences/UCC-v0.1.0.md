## Section 1: Constitutional Reasoning

### 1.1 Definitions

| Term | Definition |
|------|-----------|
| **Constitutional Inference** | A step of reasoning that derives a new claim from existing verified knowledge using a formal rule. |
| **Constitutional Derivation** | A chain of inferences that traces a claim from its conclusion back to USCP or USC. |
| **Constitutional Entailment** | The relationship between premises and conclusion: if the premises hold, the conclusion must hold. |
| **Constitutional Contradiction** | The simultaneous assertion of a claim and its negation within the same scope. Contradictions are forbidden. |

### 1.2 The Three Modes of Reasoning

UCC recognizes three modes of constitutional reasoning:

| Mode | Name | Direction | Example |
|------|------|-----------|---------|
| **Forward** | Derivation | Premises → Conclusion | "USCP exists, USC derives from USCP, therefore USC has a derivation source." |
| **Backward** | Trace | Conclusion → Premises | "UCE exists. What does UCE depend on? USC. What does USC depend on? USCP." |
| **Lateral** | Combination | Parallel claims → Joint conclusion | "UCE is verified AND UCC is verified. Therefore, the epistemic-logical core is sound." |

All three modes are constitutionally valid. All three require formal rules.

### 1.3 Axioms of Constitutional Reasoning

| # | Axiom | Derived From |
|---|-------|-------------|
| R1 | Reasoning requires identity — every proposition must be distinct and well-formed. | Identity (USCP) |
| R2 | Reasoning requires relationship — every inference connects premises to conclusion. | Relationship (USCP) |
| R3 | Reasoning requires constraint — every inference is bounded by formal rules. | Constraint (USCP) |
| R4 | Reasoning requires transformation — reasoning changes the state of knowledge (Assertion → Knowledge). | Transformation (USCP) |

All six USCP primitives are present. Additionally, R1–R6 operationalize UCE's axioms (K1–K6) for the domain of reasoning.

## Section 2: Inference Rules

### 2.1 The Constitutional Inference Engine

Every constitutional inference follows this structure:

```
Premise 1 (verified knowledge)     ┐
Premise 2 (verified knowledge)     ├─ + Rule → Conclusion
...                                │
Premise n (verified knowledge)     ┘
```

| Requirement | Rule |
|-------------|------|
| **Well-formed premises** | Every premise must be a verified claim (V3 or V4 per UCE). Assertions cannot serve as premises. |
| **Explicit rule** | Every inference must cite the rule it applies. Implicit reasoning is forbidden. |
| **Valid conclusion** | The conclusion must follow necessarily from the premises and the rule. |
| **Bounded scope** | The conclusion's scope cannot exceed the combined scope of its premises. |

### 2.2 The Five Constitutional Rules

UCC defines five formal rules for constitutional reasoning. Every valid inference uses exactly one:

| Rule | Name | Structure | Meaning |
|------|------|-----------|---------|
| **CR1** | Direct Entry | USCP/USC → Claim | A constitutional primitive or the Constitution itself may be asserted directly without further derivation. |
| **CR2** | Derivation | Verified claim → Derived claim | A new claim follows from an existing verified claim via the derivation rules (D1–D5, Part II). |
| **CR3** | Combination | Claim A + Claim B → Joint claim | Two or more verified claims combine to produce a claim that neither produces alone. Requires D3 justification. |
| **CR4** | Restriction | General claim + Condition → Scoped claim | A verified general claim, when restricted to a specific condition, yields a claim about that condition. |
| **CR5** | Abstraction | Specific claims → General claim | Multiple verified specific claims, when they share a common pattern, yield a general claim about the pattern. Requires inductive justification. |

### 2.3 Rule Application Protocol

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Identify the conclusion to be derived. |
| 2 | Identify the premises — all must be verified (V3/V4). |
| 3 | Select the applicable rule (CR1–CR5). |
| 4 | Verify that the rule's structure is satisfied. |
| 5 | State the conclusion with its scope, source, and rule citation. |
| 6 | Submit for verification (UCE V0→V3 protocol). |

No step may be skipped. The reasoning chain is an explicit, auditable artifact.

## Section 3: Derivation Logic

### 3.1 Operationalizing D1–D5

UCC operationalizes the Kernel's derivation rules (Part II) into formal logic:

| Part II Rule | UCC Formalization |
|-------------|-------------------|
| **D1: Downward only** | ∀ derivation: source_layer(target) = source_layer(premise) − 1. No layer may be derived from a higher layer. |
| **D2: No upward mutation** | ∀ amendment: target_layer(change) ≤ source_layer(change). Changes propagate downward only. |
| **D3: Merge requires justification** | ∀ combination: all parent layers must be cited as premises. No unlicensed merges. |
| **D4: Adaptation is the boundary** | ∀ claim beyond UCA: claim is Implementation, not Constitutional. UCA is the constitutional boundary. |
| **D5: Derivatives are composed** | ∀ UCD claim: claim is composed from UCA capabilities. UCD does not re-derive the kernel. |

### 3.2 Derivation Chains

Every derived claim must have a **derivation chain** — an explicit trace from the claim back to USCP or USC.

```
Conclusion ← CR2 ← Premise A ← CR2 ← Premise B ← ... ← CR1 (USCP/USC)
```

| Property | Requirement |
|----------|------------|
| **Traceable** | Every link in the chain cites its rule (CR1–CR5) and its premises. |
| **Finite** | Every chain terminates at CR1 (a constitutional primitive or USC). |
| **Non-circular** | No chain may reference itself, directly or transitively. |
| **Consistent** | No chain may produce a conclusion that contradicts an existing chain. |

### 3.3 Derivation Depth

| Depth | Name | Description |
|-------|------|-------------|
| **0** | Primitive | Directly from USCP (CR1). |
| **1** | Constitutional | From USC (CR1) or from depth-0 claims (CR2). |
| **2** | Scientific | From depth-1 claims (UCE, UCC, UCM, UCL). |
| **3+** | Derived | From depth-2+ claims. Each level adds one derivation step. |

Derivation depth is tracked in every Knowledge Record (UCE Section 5.3). Depth is informational, not hierarchical — a depth-3 claim is not less valid than a depth-1 claim, provided its chain is sound.

## Section 4: Consistency

### 4.1 Constitutional Consistency

A set of constitutional claims is **consistent** if and only if no claim contradicts any other claim within the same scope.

| Type | Definition | Detection |
|------|-----------|-----------|
| **Direct contradiction** | Claim P and Claim ¬P both verified within the same scope. | Logical check: P ∧ ¬P → ⊥. |
| **Scope contradiction** | Claim P verified for scope S, and Claim ¬P verified for scope S' where S ∩ S' ≠ ∅. | Scope intersection analysis. |
| **Derivation contradiction** | Two claims derived from the same premises via the same rules, yielding different conclusions. | Chain comparison: same inputs + same rule = same output. |

### 4.2 Consistency Rules

| # | Rule |
|---|------|
| **CON1** | The constitutional record must be consistent at all times. If a new claim creates a contradiction, it is held as Assertion (UCE Section 2.2, C5) until resolved. |
| **CON2** | Contradictions are resolved through governance (Part IV), not through logic alone. Logic detects contradictions; governance resolves them. |
| **CON3** | When a contradiction is detected, both claims are audited against their derivation chains. The claim with the shorter, more direct chain prevails provisionally. |
| **CON4** | If both claims have equal derivation depth, the claim rooted closer to USCP prevails (Part IV conflict resolution). |

### 4.3 Consistency Verification

| Step | Action |
|------|--------|
| 1 | Enumerate all verified claims in scope. |
| 2 | Check for direct contradictions (P ∧ ¬P). |
| 3 | Check for scope intersections of contradictory claims. |
| 4 | Check for derivation contradictions (same premises + same rule → different conclusions). |
| 5 | Record results. Any contradiction triggers CON1. |

Consistency verification is a constitutional requirement for every new claim entering the record.

## Section 5: Reasoning Boundaries

### 5.1 What UCC Governs

| Domain | UCC's Role |
|--------|-----------|
| **Inference validity** | Whether a conclusion follows from its premises via a valid rule. |
| **Derivation integrity** | Whether a derivation chain is complete, traceable, and non-circular. |
| **Consistency** | Whether the constitutional record is free of contradictions. |
| **Rule compliance** | Whether reasoning follows CR1–CR5, not ad hoc logic. |

### 5.2 What UCC Does NOT Govern

| Domain | Governing Science |
|--------|------------------|
| **What is knowable** | UCE (epistemic boundaries). |
| **What is true** | UCE (verification protocol). |
| **How to represent** | UCM (mathematical structures) and UCL (expression). |
| **How to implement** | UCA, UCD, and the runtime stack. |

UCC is the logic engine, not the truth engine and not the expression engine. It determines validity, not truth. A valid argument may have false premises (that is UCE's concern); an invalid argument may have true premises (that is UCC's concern).

### 5.3 Reasoning Constraints

| # | Constraint | Statement |
|---|-----------|-----------|
| RC1 | **No ad hoc reasoning.** | Every inference must use CR1–CR5. Reasoning outside these rules is not constitutional. |
| RC2 | **No hidden premises.** | Every premise must be explicit. "Obviously true" is not a constitutional justification. |
| RC3 | **No scope creep.** | A conclusion cannot exceed the scope of its premises. Generalization requires CR5 with explicit justification. |
| RC4 | **No circular chains.** | A derivation chain cannot reference itself, directly or transitively. Circularity is a structural contradiction. |
| RC5 | **No orphaned reasoning.** | Every reasoning step must be recorded in a Knowledge Record (UCE Section 5.3). Unrecorded reasoning does not exist constitutionally. |
