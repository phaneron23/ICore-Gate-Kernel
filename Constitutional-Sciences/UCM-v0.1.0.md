## Section 4: Constitutional Morphisms

### 4.1 What Is a Morphism?

A **morphism** is a structure-preserving mapping between constitutional objects. Morphisms formalize what it means for two constitutional processes to be "the same" in structure.

### 4.2 Types of Constitutional Morphisms

| Morphism | Name | Meaning | Example |
|----------|------|---------|---------|
| **Identity** | id: A → A | Every object maps to itself. | The Constitution maps to itself. |
| **Derivation** | d: A → B | B is derived from A. The structure of derivation is preserved. | USCP → USC. |
| **Composition** | d₂ ∘ d₁: A → B → C | Two derivations compose into one. | USCP → USC → UCE. |
| **Isomorphism** | f: A ↔ B | Two structures are equivalent under transformation. | Two implementations of the same primitive. |

### 4.3 Morphism Rules

| # | Rule | Statement |
|---|------|-----------|
| MR1 | **Identity exists.** | Every constitutional object has an identity morphism. |
| MR2 | **Composition is valid.** | If d₁: A → B and d₂: B → C, then d₂ ∘ d₁: A → C is a valid morphism. |
| MR3 | **Composition is associative.** | (d₃ ∘ d₂) ∘ d₁ = d₃ ∘ (d₂ ∘ d₁). |
| MR4 | **No inverse without governance.** | Morphisms are not invertible by default. Reversing a derivation requires the full governance pipeline (Part IV). |
| MR5 | **Structure preservation.** | Every morphism must preserve the constitutional properties of its source and target. |

These five rules define a **category** in the mathematical sense — the category of constitutional objects and their derivations (denoted **ICore**).
